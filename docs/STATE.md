## Mission Control globe: GPU anomaly models — 2026-11-15

- The globe's anomalies (`src/dispatch/Globe.tsx`) no longer redraw a 2D canvas and re-upload a texture every 50 ms per anomaly. They are now drawn by shaders in `src/dispatch/Anomaly.tsx` on thin spherical caps at several altitudes (parallax when the globe turns):
  - **dust storm** — modelled on orbital views of regional storms (MRO MARCI, Mars Express): an arced, towering dust front advancing along a per-storm drift direction, with sunlit boiling billows, a shadow thrown away from the scene's sun, plumes of freshly lifted dust drifting into it and occasional electrostatic flashes; behind it a pale tan veil hides the surface and thins into wind streaks. The front breathes and its lobes travel along it; the dust is carried forward. Forecast storms are washed out and faint.
  - **dust devil** — modelled on HiRISE views: a small bright vortex wandering over the area with a dusty skirt and a long thin shadow, leaving a dark winding track that fades with age.
  - **rockfall** — pale scar, dark lobed debris fan, sun-lit boulders with shadows, debris still sliding and a thin dust plume while active.
  - **solar storm** (`src/dispatch/SolarStorm.tsx`) — in addition to the existing violet atmosphere: a patchy diffuse aurora over the night side and a solar-wind particle stream deflected around a bow shock (one `Points` draw, positions computed in the vertex shader).
- Quest 2 budget measures: one shared 128² noise texture (generated once, no per-frame uploads), one shared geometry and time uniform, 3–4 draw calls per anomaly, fewer noise octaves and particles when the user agent is Quest/Oculus Browser (`src/dispatch/globeFx.ts`). The 2D zone maps still use `lib/stormArt.ts` unchanged.
- These pictures are illustrations of where an anomaly is; their size on the globe is not the measured extent, and the aurora/wind are not measurements.
- Globe auto-rotation slowed to `autoRotateSpeed` 0.08 (was 0.35).
- Verified: `tsc -b` and `npm run build` pass; headless Chrome (SwiftShader) renders storm, forecast storm, dust devil, rockfall and a critical solar storm without shader errors, and frames a few seconds apart show the front, plumes and devil moving. Frame rate on Quest 2: **NOT TESTED ON DEVICE**.

## EVA helmet scene rebuilt for a Quest 2 budget; new atmosphere and storm — 2026-11-15

The `/helmet` → `/helmet/:crewId` view (`src/eva/`) keeps its route, hazard, recall, telemetry, HUD and Supabase logic unchanged. What draws the world was rebuilt so a standalone headset has less to do, and looks different:

- **Budget** (`src/eva/quality.ts`): a Quest / Pico user agent, or `?quest` on a desktop, selects the headset tier; `?lite` is the cheapest (no shadows); `?scale=` and `?fov=` override the XR framebuffer scale and fixed foveation (headset default 1.0 and 0.45, desktop 1.15 and 0.3).
- **Ground** (`Ground.tsx`): the zone DTM is cut into 64 chunks with three levels of detail (every cell near the astronaut — the same triangles `groundY()` walks on — every 2nd and 4th further out, skirts hide the seams) and chunks outside the view are not drawn. One shader replaces the three layered meshes (zone photo, HiRISE close-up, shadow overlay): photo + close-up + fine regolith relief lit by the scene's Sun + shadows + sand streaming in a storm + haze. The DTM still supplies the geometry and the photographs only colour it.
- **Welded models** (`merge.tsx`): the outpost, science site, each butte and the suit (gloves, body, legs by joint) are built from the same small parts as before and then merged into one mesh per material. Lamp glows are one instanced draw call (`Structures.tsx` `Halos`), faint by day and swelling in dust.
- **Shadows** (`atmo.tsx` `Lights`): one shadow map redrawn only when the scene changed, or every 3rd frame on the headset tier (1024², ±42 m; desktop 2048², ±70 m, every frame).
- **Rocks** (`World.tsx`): pebbles are drawn to 26 m, stones to 55 m, boulders to 110 m on the headset tier (80 / 320 triangles each); new angular shapes with dust on top instead of flat-shaded red balls.
- **Atmosphere** (`atmo.tsx`): one set of uniforms (Sun, sky colours, haze, wind, dust 0..1) shared by sky, ground, storm and dust; a sky dome with a small solar disc and its bluish aureole, a faint streaky dust deck, and an exponential haze whose colour matches the sky in every direction. Noise comes from two small textures generated once in code (no per-pixel procedural noise).
- **Storm** (`Storm.tsx`): a dust storm seen from outside is a billowing wall over the event's area, lit by the scene's Sun, dark at the foot and ragged at the top, with dust spilling along the ground before it; a dust devil is a narrow hollow leaning vortex wandering inside its area. Inside the dust: denser gusting haze, a dark low sky with the Sun as a pale spot, dimmed ground, streaks of sand on the wind, large drifting billows, sand streaming over the ground, lamps glowing through it. All computed on the GPU. The previous "flashes in the wall" were removed (not something seen on Mars). Pictures of the event's area and of dusty air — not a simulation and not a weather observation.
- **HUD**: the visor blocks are redrawn one per frame at their own rates (heading tape 10 Hz … conditions 1 Hz) instead of all six at once.
- **Other**: the habitat now stands 12.5 m north of the landing point (the airlock ladder no longer passes through the place you start at); outer hatch detailed; `npm run dev:vr` added as an alias of `dev:quest` (`package-lock.json` untouched, no dependency added); in a headset session 72 Hz is requested when the browser offers it, and a governor raises foveation step by step if frames are being dropped.

Owner request: being inside a storm should feel dangerous, and the sound needed work. Presentation only: routes, hazards, recall, telemetry and the suit model are unchanged.

- **One shared weather state** (`src/eva/wind.ts`): the scene writes the dust level, the gust strength, a passing squall and static discharges every frame (`atmo.tsx`); the sound, the visor and the screen view read them, so what is seen and heard happens together.
- **Squalls**: inside a storm a squall passes every ~19 and ~31 s. For a few seconds the haze closes in from roughly 30 m to about 12 m (the habitat a dozen metres away dissolves), the light drops by half, the sand flies faster and longer, with bigger dark grains among it. Thick dust is also darker than before overall.
- **Visor** (`Storm.tsx` `VisorGrit`, `EvaScene.tsx` `XrHud`): grains flash as short scratches on the glass (a few a second, a hail in a squall); dust slowly settles on the visor, thickest at the rim, and clears after you leave (drawn in the existing vignette pass, under the display blocks so the readouts stay clear); the display flickers for ~0.1 s on a static discharge.
- **Screen mode only**: the view is buffeted slightly in a squall. Never in the headset — the head pose stays the user's own.
- **Sound** (`audio.ts`, all synthesised): the single wind noise is replaced by a low rumble, a howl and a thin whistle that rise with each gust, sand hissing and pattering on the visor, a thud when a squall arrives, the crackle of static discharges, interference on the radio tones and on recorded calls, a fan that labours, faster and louder breathing and an audible pulse deep in the dust. A limiter on the output keeps storm, alarm and voice from clipping together.
- **Fixed on the way**: in screen mode the forearms were aimed with a world-space `lookAt` although they hang in the camera's frame, so their pose depended on where you stood and faced (`SuitBody.tsx` `aim`). Gloves in VR were not affected.

## Alerts and calls on the visor — 2026-11-15

Owner request: the notifications from the ship and the hazard alerts had to fit the field of view and the style of the helmet display. Before, the visor showed one line with the alert's title cut at 34 characters; the text of a call from the ship or mission control was only spoken, never shown; where a hazard lay was not shown at all.

- **Alert block** (`src/eva/xrHud.ts` `alertsBlock`, replaces `message`; used by the headset patches and the screen view): a short list in the manner of a crew alerting display, in the HUD's own type and colours (red WARNING, amber CAUTION, plain phosphor for advisories and calls; no filled panels). The most important entry is framed and carries what the astronaut needs:
  - a hazard with an area — which way to look and how far its edge is (`◂ 018°  593 m`; `▲` ahead, `▼` behind; "you are in it" inside), and on the route how far along it;
  - a call from the ship or mission control — sender, subject, age, and the text itself wrapped to three lines; after 25 s it folds to one dim line (it has been read out too);
  - a recall — the reason, then "preparing the return route" (not shown before), the distance to the airlock once the route is in, "hold position / contact the ship" if none was found.
  The other alerts follow as one short line each (as many as fit, then "+N more"). A new entry's frame flashes for 1.6 s; only a WARNING keeps blinking. Titles are cut by measured width with an ellipsis instead of at a fixed character count.
- **Field of view**: the block's first line stays ~16.5° above the line of sight (where the single line was), the rest grows downward to ~9°; nothing is wider than ±17°, inside the sharp part of a Quest 2 lens. The block is 640 × 162 px at 21 px per degree.
- **Heading tape**: a diamond marks the bearing of the two nearest hazards (red / amber), pinned to the tape's end with an arrow when outside ±45°.
- **Notices** (`useEva.ts` `notice`, in the view for 12 s): events that were sound-only now also appear as a line — a new route from the ship, going off the route and rerouting, the route home updated, no route from here. No new translation keys: existing `evahud.*`, `hazards.*` and `voice.*` strings are reused (en / ru / kk).
- Display only, as before: nothing to press on the visor; acknowledging with A / Enter and the alarm, radio tones and voice are unchanged, as is what counts as an alert (`lib/hazards.ts`).
- **Preview without the database** (`xrPreview.ts`): `?simhud=message | hazard | recall | many` puts sample alerts on this browser's display only — no sound, no recall, nothing stored or sent.

Verified: `tsc -b`, `npm run build`, no lint errors in the changed files; the block rendered in headless Chrome at one Quest 2 eye's resolution (1832 × 1920, `?xrview=1`) for the four previews in English and Russian, and in the screen view. Real `events` rows go through the same code but none was created in this check; Kazakh was not rendered. **NOT TESTED ON DEVICE**: legibility of the 15–18 px lines through the lenses, whether three lines of a call ~9–14° above the line of sight are comfortable to read while walking, the frame flash in peripheral vision.

## Consumables spent by the walk, planner profile, guidance check, quieter alerts — 2026-11-15

**Suit consumables** (`src/lib/consumables.ts`, `src/eva/suit.ts`, `useEva.ts`) — SIMULATED, not suit data and not a safety calculation. Before, oxygen, power and cooling water drained by the clock at an 8–9 hour rate, so in a demo walked at 8× pace they never visibly moved.
- They are now spent by the metre walked: a full suit lasts 8 h (oxygen), 9 h (power), 8.5 h (water) of steady suited walking on level ground at the planner's own pace, i.e. about 7 % of oxygen per level kilometre. A slope scales oxygen and water by the energy cost of walking it — the polynomial Minetti et al. fitted to measured walking costs (≈2× at +10 % grade, 3× at +20 %, 0.45× at −10 %, rising again on steep descents) — and power by the time the slope takes. Thick dust adds 15 % oxygen and 50 % power. Standing still costs a little by the clock.
- The helmet shows what is expected to be left at the airlock (the least of the three after the rest of the way out and the way back): `MARGIN` in the navigation block is now that percentage. Below 25 % a CAUTION, below 10 % a WARNING, each once, on the alert list with its tone. Suit status follows.
- At the airlock with no walk under way (within 15 m of the landing point, standby or EVA complete) the suit is topped up at ~1.5 % a second; a new EVA still starts full.
- Checked: a 900 m preview route on autopilot used 6.5 % oxygen, 5.9 % power, 6.1 % water (it used ~0 before); with 31 % oxygen and a 1.5 km route the display showed `WARNING  RESERVE FOR THE WAY BACK 9%`.

**Ship planner** (`src/components/RouteProfile.tsx`, `src/pages/Ship.tsx`):
- under the route's figures, the elevation along the route (the DTM heights stored with each route point), coloured by slope in five classes — steep descent / descent / level / climb / steep climb at ±2.5° and ±8°, blue to red through a neutral — with the vertical exaggeration stated, a crosshair readout (elevation, slope, distance, oxygen used by then) and the hovered point marked on the map; an EVA in progress shows how far the astronaut is;
- the selected plan's route and a new unsent route are drawn on the map in the same slope colours on a dark casing (unsent: dashed); a return route keeps its blue dashes; legend added;
- "Suit use: out, 1 h at the target, back": the three consumables the walk is expected to take, amber above 80 %, red with a note above 100 %. The same model the helmet runs.
- Checked in headless Chrome on the project's existing plan (saved return route, and a draft generated but **not sent**), English and Russian. Kazakh strings were added but not rendered.
